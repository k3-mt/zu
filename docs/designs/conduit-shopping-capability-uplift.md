# Upstream primitives for Conduit shopping (Zu companion)

**Status:** proposed (2026-07-06). **Owner:** Conduit + Zu. **Source:** the (git-ignored) Conduit Skill Library + Shopping Design Brief.

Conduit is building a shopping capability — a library of 27 skills (20 agent-work, 7 platform) that operate real UK retail sites *as the user*, plus a first-class product-attribute reasoning layer that turns "add to cart" into "buy the right thing for a child with a nut allergy." Conduit is a private monorepo built **on** a pinned Zu; it imports Zu and never forks it. This document is the Zu-facing companion to the Conduit shopping PRD: it enumerates *exactly* which of Zu's mechanical guarantees the shopping capability rests on, so those touchpoints are precise, testable, and minimal — and it draws the line, per `ZU-NOT`, at the point where Conduit's domain logic begins and Zu's core must not follow. It is written in the idiom of [`zu-upstream-conformance.md`](../../zu-upstream-conformance.md): RFC-2119 levels, every touchpoint traced to a conformance tag, a SHIPPED/GAP status, and a named offline proof guarded by `packages/zu-core/tests/test_conformance_matrix.py`. The trusted/untrusted split it references is enumerated in [`docs/TCB.md`](../TCB.md).

The two Conduit invariants this doc must never erode are (1) the **content-free action surface** — the model driving the browser sees each control's structure (role, accessible name, a few states), never the page's prose, so a page cannot inject instructions; and (2) the **deterministic commit boundary** — the browser never presses pay/place-order/confirm; a vault plus a phone WebAuthn approval bound to the exact amount does. Both are Conduit's to hold. Zu's job is only the substrate beneath them.

---

## 1. The dependency thesis

Conduit's shopping skills are ordinary agent runs with three unusual demands: they spend real money, they run unattended on a schedule, and a wrong product can be a safety incident (`REPL-2` infant-formula stage, `REPL-4` OTC quantity limits, an allergen substitution in `GROC-1`). None of that is buildable unless Zu mechanically **interposes** a pre-execution gate on every reach toward the world, **escalates** a doubtful step to a human with ground-truth Zu-held content, **records** a tamper-evident chain, and lets Conduit **extend** it as plugins without editing the core. Those are exactly the guarantees the conformance spec already pins down. This document does not ask Zu for anything new for the *first* wave of shopping skills: every load-bearing touchpoint is already **SHIPPED** and has a named offline proof. It exists to (a) make the shopping capability's dependence on those primitives explicit and re-testable as Conduit grows, and (b) hold the `ZU-NOT` line hard — the product-attribute schema, the fourteen-allergen enumeration, the household constraint profile, the hard-filter/soft-rank matcher, and the cross-store product-knowledge cache are **all Conduit domain logic** and Zu must not absorb them. Per `ZU-NOT`, Zu provides the mechanism; Conduit keeps the judgment.

A single sentence captures the ask: *can Conduit gate every purchase pre-execution, escalate a safety-critical uncertainty to a human on Zu-held ground truth, run in-scope on a schedule without a fresh approval, and prove after the fact that it acted within a revocable mandate — using Zu mechanisms it extends as plugins, without forking the core?* The answer today is yes; the table below is the receipt.

### 2.1 T1 — the escalate path is the safety spine (`ZU-CD-1/2/5`)

This is the one touchpoint the Shopping Design Brief leans on hardest. The brief's rule is: when an attribute is **safety-critical AND uncertain** — a missing allergen field on a substitute, an ambiguous formula stage, an OTC quantity at a legal limit — the agent must escalate, never guess. Conduit owns the *judgment* (is this decision safety-critical? is the datum missing or ambiguous?) — that classifier is Conduit's `judgment.py` HIGH/LOW and its `Decision.ASK_HUMAN`. Zu owns the *mechanism* beneath it: `_pause_for_human` renders the **literal invocation the harness will execute** (`ZU-CD-1` — the possibly-tricked model cannot narrate a friendlier version of what is about to happen), and `run_task(resume_from=…)` binds the human's resolution to that exact call so the run cannot approve-then-swap (`ZU-CD-2`), with taint and accumulated caps preserved across the pause (`ZU-CD-5`).

- **Why it must be Zu's:** a page-derived attribute is untrusted; a substitution proposed off it is a policy output. If the escalation showed a policy-authored summary, a poisoned product page could make "regular milk, £0.80" read as "oat milk, dairy-free" at the approval card. Zu's ground-truth rendering is what makes the human a real check rather than a rubber stamp.
- **Failure mode:** policy-narrated escalation → the canonical allergen-substitution failure ships silently behind a reassuring card.
- **Boundary:** Zu does not decide *when* to escalate for shopping. The trigger — "safety-critical attribute missing on a hard constraint" — is Conduit's, defined in the product-attribute reasoning doc.

### 2.2 T2 — the spend cap is enforced beneath the policy (`ZU-CORE-2` + `ZU-CD-4/8`)

PLAT-2 says "the agent cannot exceed the cap." That is only true if the cap is checked somewhere the policy cannot reach. Conduit **defines** the cap as a `Scope`/`Grant` limit on the mandate; Zu **enforces** it in three composable places, all beneath the policy: (1) the pre-execution `InvocationGate` reads durable per-grant state (`GrantStore`) so a per-period cap sees spend-so-far, not just the single call (`ZU-CD-4`); (2) the credential broker refuses an over-cumulative use **atomically** via `incr_if_below`, so two concurrent shopping runs cannot both slip under the same cap (`ZU-CD-8`); (3) a period cap declared as a `SPEND_VELOCITY` invariant compiles to a `Monitor` folding the log (`ZU-RAIL-6`). A per-period cap (PLAT-2's own example — "never spend more than £150 a week") spans several runs — a scheduled `GROC-1`, a `REPL-1` nappy reorder, a `GIFT-2` — so the gate must fold spend-so-far rather than judge each call alone; a stateless per-call check would wave each order through sub-threshold while the week's total ran past. `OPT-3`, which routes a whole basket to the single cheapest store, is the large one-shot order the per-task cap bounds (`OPT-2` only prices and recommends — it never spends).

- **Failure mode:** a per-call-only check → many sub-threshold orders drain past a weekly cap (the slow-drip drain `ZU-CD-4` exists to stop).

### 2.3 T3/T5 — the mandate is auditable and revocable (`ZU-AUDIT-*`, `ZU-CD-8`)

Every attribute decision the brief requires to be auditable ("which attributes, from which source, and why this SKU") lands as consumer fields under `payload["ctx"]` on Zu's hash chain (`ZU-AUDIT-3`), and every card use is bound to the consent that justified it (`ZU-AUDIT-5`). Conduit builds the browsable receipt and the signed per-order record on top; Zu guarantees the substrate is append-only and tamper-evident (`ZU-AUDIT-1`) and records the *why* — which gate rule allowed a step or which human approved it (`ZU-AUDIT-2`). PLAT-7's revoke rides `ZU-CD-8`: once a mandate's `Grant` is revoked, the broker refuses the next use and logs a `harness.defense.blocked`, so "stop — cancel everything" is mechanical, not a request the policy may ignore.

### 2.4 T6 — content_view is an acquisition *input*, not the schema (`ZU-CD-3`)

The Shopping Design Brief's first design question is acquisition: how does the agent obtain weight, pack size, allergens, ingredients when the data is inconsistent per site? Conduit's primary path is the retailer's own structured commerce metadata (JSON-LD `Product`/`Offer`), read deterministically by Conduit code — that is Conduit domain logic and lives in Conduit. Where a site publishes an attribute only as pack/label prose, Zu's `content_view` (#41) is the sanctioned way to read it: an **untrusted** reading projection the model reaches only through `TrustedFrame`, which renders content as fenced DATA, never instructions — preserving the content-free-surface invariant on the reading side too. Critically, a `content_view` read **taints the run** (`ZU-CD-3`): journalling provenance and a content hash, never the body. That taint is what makes any subsequent money step re-escalate under `ZU-CD-4`/T1 — reading a persuasive product page cannot silently authorise a purchase.

- **Boundary (the important one):** Zu emits untrusted content units with a provenance tag. **Normalising** them into a canonical product record, extracting the fourteen regulated allergens, scoring per-match confidence, and deciding a substitution is constraint-preserving are **all Conduit's**, per `ZU-NOT`. Zu must not learn what "may contain nuts" means. That normalisation is specified in Conduit's product-attribute-reasoning PRD (`02-product-attribute-reasoning.md`, in the Conduit private repo — not linked here, as it is not a Zu-repo path); it extends Conduit's `structured_price.py`/`situation.py`/`vocabulary.py`, never Zu.

## 3. Candidate gaps to watch

Nothing in the first shopping wave requires a Zu change. Two places *could* surface a genuine upstream gap as the capability hardens; each would be filed as a `zu_report_gap` and adopted via **file → fix → adopt → delete**, and each must be a **generic** mechanism — never Conduit's domain logic.

1. **A reusable structured-product-metadata reading projection (watch).** Conduit today reads JSON-LD `Product`/`Offer` for price deterministically in its own code (the `structured_price.py` pattern). The *same* structured object carries allergens, ingredients, pack size, nutrition, and age-grading. If — across the proving grounds — a **generic, content-free projection of structured commerce metadata** proves to be a primitive worth sharing (the mechanical extraction of typed fields from JSON-LD / OpenGraph / microdata, with provenance, no domain vocabulary), that is a candidate Zu reading projection sibling to `content_view`, filed upstream. The line: Zu would extract *typed key/value facts with provenance*; Zu would **not** know that `milk` is one of fourteen regulated allergens or that a hard constraint filters a candidate. The vocabulary stays a Conduit data table (`vocabulary.py` is the model), not an engine edit.
2. **`ReplayArbiter` sensitivity for shopping drift (watch).** The standing-mandate rail (T8) escalates a consequential replay divergence to a human via `ZU-RAIL-3`. If the shopping vertical needs a divergence signal Zu's arbiter port cannot express structurally, that is a `ZU-RAIL` extension request — but the diff metric and thresholds stay Conduit's per `ZU-RAIL-3`'s own division of labour; only the *hook* would be upstream.

The discipline is the project's: if Zu can't do something generic that the capability genuinely needs, that is a capability gap to fix upstream, not to hack around — and only the generic mechanism crosses the boundary.

## 4. Explicit non-asks (`ZU-NOT`)

To keep Zu's trusted core minimal, the shopping capability explicitly asks Zu **not** to absorb the following. Each is Conduit domain logic and belongs in `conduit_contracts` / `conduit_core` / `conduit_api`:

- **The canonical product-attribute schema and the fourteen-allergen / dietary enumerations.** Zu stays product-agnostic. Allergens and dietary flags as first-class enumerated fields are a Conduit data table, extending Conduit's `vocabulary.py`. (Aligns with `ZU-NOT-2`: Zu must not know about instruments; by the same logic, not about products.)
- **The household constraint profile and the hard-vs-soft line.** Which constraints are hard filters (allergens, dietary rules, age-restricted items) versus soft ranking weights (brand, size, price) is Conduit's, attached to the mandate. Zu supplies the `Grant`/`Scope` shapes and the gate hook; it does not model the profile (`ZU-NOT-1`, `ZU-NOT-3`).
- **The hard-filter-then-soft-rank matcher and constraint-preserving substitution.** Filtering ineligible candidates and re-running the filter over replacements is Conduit's scope-checker-shaped logic, supplied as a Conduit `Validator` — not one Zu authors (`ZU-NOT-3`).
- **The cross-store product-knowledge cache.** The normalised-attribute cache that powers both correct shopping and the `OPT-*` price optimiser is Conduit's system of record for products, not a Zu store. Zu owns the audit log of *actions*, not a catalogue of *products*.

Keeping these in Conduit is what lets Zu's TCB stay the small, auditable set enumerated in [`docs/TCB.md`](../TCB.md). A shopping capability that pushed any of them into Zu would enlarge every downstream consumer's trusted base for a benefit only Conduit gets (`ZU-NOT-5`).

## 5. Acceptance

This companion is satisfied when, for the shopping capability:

- Every touchpoint in §2 stays **SHIPPED** with its named proof green under `packages/zu-core/tests/test_conformance_matrix.py` — no shopping skill introduces a new Zu MUST that lacks an offline proof.
- No Conduit shopping change requires editing `zu-core`; new surfaces arrive as plugins behind existing ports (`ZU-EXT-1`).
- Any acquisition primitive that graduates to Zu (§3) ships as a **generic, content-free** projection with its own offline proof and carries **zero** allergen/dietary/product vocabulary — the `ZU-NOT` line in §4 holds.
- The two Conduit invariants remain intact upstream: Zu never renders page prose to the model as instructions (reads go through `TrustedFrame` and taint the run), and Zu never presses the commit — the money step is the broker plus a human approval bound to the exact amount, beneath the policy.

**Open question for maintainers:** is the structured-commerce-metadata projection of §3.1 worth pre-empting as a shared `content_view` sibling now, or better left until a second consumer needs it? Conduit's position is the latter — build it when a second consumer proves it generic — consistent with `ZU-NOT` and the "smallest set of primitives" principle of the conformance spec.
